International Student Guide to Australia: Universities, Visa & Living Costs 2027

A practical 2027 guide for international students planning to study in Australia. Covers top university options, student visa requirements, estimated living costs, and pre-departure tips to help you prepare for academic life Down Under.

More than 700,000 international students were enrolled in Australian institutions in early 2027, according to the Department of Education. This figure reflects a steady post-pandemic recovery and the country’s enduring appeal as a study destination. The Australian Government’s $37.8 billion international education sector continues to invest heavily in student support services, streamlined visa processing, and post-study work rights.

Whether you are researching undergraduate pathways, a master’s program, or a doctoral degree, Australia offers a regulated, high-quality education system. The Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) and the Australian Skills Quality Authority (ASQA) enforce consistent academic standards across all registered providers. This guide helps you navigate the key steps: choosing a university, understanding the visa framework, budgeting realistically, and preparing for arrival.

Choosing an Australian University and Course

Australia is home to 43 universities, including 37 public institutions, two international universities, and four private providers. The Group of Eight (Go8) universities—Melbourne, Sydney, Queensland, ANU, UNSW, Monash, Adelaide, and Western Australia—attract a significant share of international research students. In the 2027 QS World University Rankings, five Go8 members sit inside the global top 50, with the University of Melbourne leading at 22nd.

Course accreditation matters more than brand prestige alone. If you plan to work in a regulated profession—engineering, nursing, accounting, teaching, or law—confirm that your program is recognised by the relevant professional body. Engineers Australia, for instance, maintains a public list of accredited courses. Enrolling in an unaccredited program can delay or block your eligibility for a post-study work visa under subclass 485.

Regional university campuses offer a tangible advantage. The Australian Government’s migration settings incentivise study in regional areas with extended post-study work rights and additional points under the General Skilled Migration points test. Universities such as the University of Wollongong, Deakin (Geelong campus), James Cook, and the University of Tasmania deliver research-intensive programs outside the major metropolitan centres of Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane.

When comparing courses, look beyond the tuition fee. Examine the student-to-staff ratio, industry placement hours, and graduate employment rates. The 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey showed that international graduates from health-related fields reported a full-time employment rate of 78.2% within four months of completing their degree. Use the government’s Course Seeker platform to filter by institution, location, and ATAR profile where relevant.

Understanding the Student Visa (Subclass 500) in 2027

The Student visa (subclass 500) remains the primary pathway for full-time international students. In 2027, the Department of Home Affairs continues to apply the Genuine Student (GS) requirement, which replaced the previous Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) framework. You must demonstrate that you understand the demands of living and studying in Australia and that your chosen course advances your academic or career objectives.

Key requirements include a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from a CRICOS-registered provider, adequate health insurance through Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC), and sufficient financial capacity. The financial requirement for 2027 is set at a minimum of AUD $24,505 per year for living costs for a single student, plus course fees and travel expenses. If you bring a partner or dependant, the threshold increases.

English language proficiency remains a core condition. The Department accepts IELTS, TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, and Cambridge English Advanced. Minimum scores vary by course level and provider, but as a benchmark, an overall IELTS score of 5.5 is typical for packaged foundation programs, while a minimum of 6.5 overall is standard for direct entry into most undergraduate and postgraduate degrees. Always verify the exact score required on your provider’s CRICOS page.

Processing times for the subclass 500 visa averaged 24 days for the Higher Education sector in the first quarter of 2027, according to the Department’s public data. Apply as soon as you receive your CoE, and ensure all documents—especially the GS statement and financial evidence—are complete. Incomplete applications remain the single biggest cause of delays. The visa application charge for the primary applicant is AUD $1,600, with additional charges for dependants.

Cost of Living and Budgeting for Australia

Living costs vary significantly between cities, but the 2027 benchmark of AUD $24,505 per year is a realistic floor for a single student sharing accommodation. Sydney and Melbourne sit at the higher end of the spectrum, while Adelaide, Perth, and Hobart generally offer lower rent. A shared apartment in inner Sydney can cost AUD $350–$500 per week, whereas a comparable room in Adelaide might cost AUD $200–$300 per week.

Groceries and eating out are the next largest variable expense. A monthly grocery budget of AUD $400–$600 is typical for a single person cooking at home. Meal delivery and restaurant dining inflate costs quickly; a modest café lunch averages AUD $18–$25, and a dinner main course in a mid-range restaurant costs AUD $28–$40. Use supermarket loyalty apps and student discount cards to reduce recurring costs.

Transport costs depend on your city and commute. Most state capitals offer concession fares for international students who hold a valid student ID. In Melbourne, a myki student pass caps daily travel at approximately AUD $5.30 in Zone 1. Sydney’s Opal card offers a 50% discount for eligible tertiary students. Budget AUD $40–$70 per week for public transport if you travel to campus most days.

Other unavoidable costs include OSHC premiums, which average AUD $550–$750 per year for a single policyholder, and utilities for off-campus housing. Electricity, gas, water, and internet typically add AUD $30–$60 per week in a shared household. Build a buffer of at least AUD $2,000 for initial expenses—bond deposit, temporary accommodation, and basic furniture—before your first rental income arrives.

Working While You Study

Student visa holders can work up to 48 hours per fortnight during the academic term and unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. This cap was reinstated in mid-2023 and remains in place for 2027. The national minimum wage sits at AUD $23.23 per hour, with casual roles often attracting a 25% loading. Hospitality, retail, aged care, and tutoring are common industries for international students.

Tax file number (TFN) registration is essential before you start any paid work. Apply online through the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) as soon as you arrive. Without a TFN, your employer must withhold tax at the top marginal rate. You may also be eligible for a tax refund at the end of the financial year if your total income falls below the tax-free threshold of AUD $18,200.

Workplace rights for international students are identical to those of Australian residents. The Fair Work Ombudsman enforces minimum pay rates, penalty rates, and safe working conditions. If an employer offers cash-in-hand payments below the minimum wage or pressures you to work beyond your visa hours, you can report them anonymously. Universities often provide free legal and employment advisory services through their student unions.

Balancing work and study requires realistic planning. A 20-hour per week commitment at minimum wage generates roughly AUD $465 per week before tax, enough to cover rent and groceries in most cities. Prioritise your academic timetable first; failing a unit because of excessive work hours can jeopardise your CoE and visa status. Use the first few weeks on campus to settle into your study rhythm before taking on a job.

Pre-Departure Checklist and First Weeks on Campus

Start your pre-departure planning at least three months before your course begins. Secure your passport validity for the entire intended stay, accept your offer formally, and pay the required tuition deposit to receive your CoE. Arrange OSHC coverage that starts from the day you land, not the day your course commences. Book temporary accommodation—university residential colleges, homestay, or a short-term rental—for your first two to four weeks.

Health and medical preparation should not be overlooked. Australia has stringent biosecurity laws; declare any food, plant material, or animal products on your incoming passenger card. If you take prescription medication, bring a letter from your doctor and keep medicines in original packaging. Some universities require additional health screenings for clinical placement courses, so check your faculty’s requirements early.

Orientation Week, commonly called O-Week, runs one to two weeks before lectures begin. Attend the sessions organised by your faculty and the international student support office. You will receive your student ID card, set up your university IT account, and learn about academic integrity expectations. This is also when you can join student clubs and societies—a proven way to build a social network and ease the cultural transition.

In your first month, open an Australian bank account. The big four banks—Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ, and NAB—allow you to apply online before arrival and verify your identity in-branch. You will need your passport, CoE, and an Australian address. Also apply for your TFN, register with a local doctor under your OSHC policy, and download essential apps such as your university’s learning management system, the local public transport planner, and emergency alert services.

Safety and emergency contacts should be saved in your phone. The national emergency number is 000 for police, fire, and ambulance. The Lifeline crisis support line (13 11 14) offers 24/7 confidential help if you feel overwhelmed. Every university provides free, confidential counselling services; using them is a sign of self-awareness, not weakness. Australia is generally a safe country, but take standard urban precautions—lock your bike, avoid unlit areas at night, and never share your bank PIN.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bring my family on a student visa? Yes. Your partner and dependent children can be included in your subclass 500 application. Your partner can work up to 48 hours per fortnight, and school-aged children must enrol in school. You must demonstrate additional financial capacity for each dependant.

What happens if my course finishes earlier than my visa expiry? You must either depart Australia, apply for a new visa (such as the subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa), or enrol in another CRICOS-registered course and obtain a new CoE. Overstaying without a valid visa triggers serious consequences, including a three-year exclusion period.

How do I renew my student visa if I extend my course? Apply for a new subclass 500 visa before your current visa expires. You will need a new CoE, updated OSHC, and evidence of ongoing financial capacity. If you apply while holding a valid visa, you will receive a Bridging visa A that maintains your lawful status during processing.

Is the subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa still available in 2027? Yes. Bachelor’s graduates can receive a two-year post-study work stream visa. Master’s by coursework graduates receive three years, and doctoral graduates receive four years. Graduates from regional institutions may qualify for an additional one to two years.
