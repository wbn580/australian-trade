How to Become a Licensed Real Estate Agent in Australia: A Complete Guide for 2026

A step-by-step guide to becoming a fully licensed real estate agent in Australia. Covers education pathways, state-by-state licensing requirements, 2026 fee structures, and career pathways for aspiring property professionals.

The Australian property market continues to expand rapidly, with the real estate services sector contributing over $184 billion to the national economy in 2025 according to the Australian Bureau of Statistics. Demand for qualified professionals remains strong, with the Property Council of Australia projecting a 12% growth in licensed agent positions through 2028. Whether you are considering a career change or entering the workforce for the first time, understanding the licensing pathway is essential. This guide breaks down the exact steps you need to take in 2026 to secure your real estate license and begin practising legally across any Australian state or territory.

Understanding Australia’s Real Estate Licensing Framework

Australia does not operate under a single national licensing system for real estate agents. Instead, each state and territory maintains its own regulatory body and specific requirements. The Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) provides the foundation, but state-level variations mean you must carefully check local rules before enrolling in any course.

The primary qualification across most jurisdictions is the Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice (CPP41419). This replaced older qualifications in 2022 and now serves as the industry standard. Some states require additional components beyond this certificate, including supervised work experience periods and separate licence examinations. In New South Wales, for example, you must complete a Certificate of Registration before progressing to a full licence, while Western Australia demands a mandatory triennial licence renewal with ongoing professional development points.

Regulatory oversight falls to agencies such as NSW Fair Trading, Consumer Affairs Victoria, the Queensland Office of Fair Trading, and Consumer Protection WA. Each body publishes updated licensing guides annually, typically in July, so checking their official websites ensures you work with the most current information for 2026.

Step One: Meeting the Entry Requirements

Before enrolling in any real estate course, you must confirm you meet basic eligibility criteria. These requirements are consistent across most Australian states with minor variations.

Minimum age stands at 18 years across all jurisdictions. You must be an Australian citizen, permanent resident, or hold a visa with unrestricted work rights. Some states, including Victoria and Queensland, also require you to be a fit and proper person, meaning you cannot have certain criminal convictions, particularly those involving fraud, dishonesty, or violence. A National Police Certificate issued within the last 12 months is typically required as part of your licence application.

Financial considerations matter too. The 2026 course fees for the Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice range from $1,200 to $3,800 depending on the registered training organisation (RTO) you select. Some providers offer payment plans, and a limited number of government-subsidised places exist through Smart and Skilled in NSW or Skills First in Victoria. You should budget an additional $500 to $1,200 for licence application fees, police checks, and professional indemnity insurance setup costs.

Step Two: Choosing the Right Education Pathway

Selecting a quality RTO is perhaps the most critical decision in your licensing journey. The Australian Skills Quality Authority (ASQA) regulates all providers, but course delivery methods and support levels vary significantly.

Online learning dominates the market in 2026, with most students opting for self-paced digital courses. This flexibility suits those balancing current employment with study, though it demands strong self-discipline. Typical completion times range from six to twelve months for part-time learners. Some RTOs offer accelerated pathways, allowing dedicated students to finish in as little as three months.

Face-to-face training remains available through TAFE colleges and select private providers in major cities. These structured programs suit learners who benefit from direct instructor interaction and peer networking. TAFE NSW, for instance, runs regular intakes across its Sydney, Newcastle, and Wollongong campuses, with 2026 course fees set at $1,980 for domestic students.

When comparing providers, verify their ASQA registration status and check student completion rates. The MySkills website, maintained by the Australian Government, publishes independent provider performance data. Look for RTOs with completion rates above 70% and strong student satisfaction scores. Key units within the Certificate IV cover property sales, property management, trust accounting, and legal and ethical compliance — ensure your chosen provider covers all mandatory units thoroughly.

Step Three: State-Specific Licensing Requirements for 2026

Understanding your target state’s unique process prevents costly mistakes and delays. Below is a breakdown of the major markets.

New South Wales

NSW operates a two-tier system. You begin with a Certificate of Registration, which allows you to work as an assistant agent under supervision. To obtain this, you must complete the Assistant Agent Property Course (five units from the Certificate IV). After 12 months of supervised experience, you can apply for a Class 2 Real Estate Agent Licence, which requires completing the full Certificate IV and passing a licensing examination administered by NSW Fair Trading. The 2026 exam fee is $285 per attempt.

Victoria

Victoria requires direct completion of the Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice through an approved RTO. No preliminary registration exists. After earning your qualification, you apply to the Business Licensing Authority (BLA) for an Estate Agent’s Licence. The BLA conducts a thorough probity check, and the 2026 application fee is $812.90. If you intend to operate your own agency, you will need the full licence; those working as employee agents can operate under a Corporation Licence held by their employer.

Queensland

The Queensland Office of Fair Trading requires the Certificate IV plus a Real Estate Licence Application that includes proof of qualification, a criminal history check, and a declaration of financial soundness. Queensland issues several licence types: Real Estate Agent Licence (full), Resident Letting Agent Licence, and Auctioneer Licence. The full agent licence application fee in 2026 is $1,354.40 for a three-year term. You must also complete a mandatory CPD program each year to maintain your licence.

Western Australia

WA’s Department of Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety mandates the Certificate IV followed by a Licence Examination. You must also demonstrate two years of practical experience working under a licensed agent before qualifying for a full Real Estate and Business Agent Licence. The 2026 triennial licence fee is $1,035.70, and agents must complete 10 CPD points annually, including mandatory activities in trust accounting and legal updates.

Career Pathways After Licensing

Once licensed, multiple career directions open up. Most new agents begin in residential sales or property management. Residential sales agents typically work on commission, with the Real Estate Institute of Australia reporting average first-year earnings of $55,000 to $75,000 including commissions in 2025. Experienced agents in premium markets like Sydney’s Eastern Suburbs or Melbourne’s Bayside regularly exceed $150,000 annually.

Property management offers more stable income through salary-based roles. Entry-level property managers earn $60,000 to $80,000, with senior portfolio managers commanding $90,000 to $120,000 in major cities. The 2026 rental market remains tight, with national vacancy rates hovering around 1.2% according to SQM Research, meaning skilled property managers are in high demand.

Commercial real estate represents another pathway, typically requiring additional on-the-job training. Commercial agents handle office buildings, retail spaces, and industrial properties. This sector often attracts professionals with backgrounds in finance or business, and earnings potential can be substantial, with top performers in institutional sales earning $250,000-plus annually.

Maintaining Your Licence and Continuing Professional Development

Licensing is not a one-time achievement. Every Australian state requires ongoing Continuing Professional Development (CPD) to ensure agents remain current with legislation, market practices, and ethical standards.

CPD requirements vary by state but typically involve six to twelve hours of approved training annually. Topics often mandated include trust accounting updates, anti-money laundering compliance, privacy law changes, and ethical conduct refreshers. Failure to complete CPD can result in licence suspension or non-renewal.

Most RTOs and industry bodies like the Real Estate Institute of Australia (REIA) offer CPD programs throughout the year. Many sessions are now delivered via webinar, making compliance manageable even for busy professionals. Annual CPD costs range from $300 to $800 depending on your state and chosen provider. Tracking your CPD points through your state regulator’s online portal is your responsibility, and audits do occur — keep all completion certificates for at least five years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a fully licensed real estate agent in Australia?

The timeline varies by state and your study pace. In NSW, expect approximately 18 to 24 months from starting your Assistant Agent course to receiving a full Class 2 licence. In Victoria, dedicated full-time students can complete the Certificate IV in three to six months, with licence processing adding another four to eight weeks. Western Australia’s experience requirement extends the timeline to roughly three years for a full licence.

Can I transfer my real estate licence between states?

Australia has a mutual recognition framework, but it is not automatic. If you hold a current licence in one state and wish to work in another, you can apply for mutual recognition through the target state’s regulator. You will typically need to complete a short bridging course covering state-specific legislation and pay an application fee. The process usually takes four to six weeks.

What is the difference between a real estate agent and a buyer’s agent?

A real estate agent represents the seller (vendor) in a property transaction, marketing the property and negotiating on the seller’s behalf. A buyer’s agent exclusively represents the purchaser, searching for suitable properties and negotiating purchase terms. Buyer’s agents require a separate Buyer’s Agent Licence in most states, with additional training in property valuation and buyer advocacy. The demand for buyer’s agents has grown sharply since 2023, particularly in competitive markets.

Do I need a university degree to become a real estate agent?

No. The Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice is a vocational qualification and does not require prior university study. However, degrees in property economics, business, or finance can provide a competitive advantage, particularly for commercial real estate roles. Some universities offer Bachelor of Property degrees that embed the Certificate IV, allowing graduates to exit with both a degree and a licence-ready qualification.
