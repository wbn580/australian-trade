How to Become a Licensed Migration Agent in Australia in 2026: A Step-by-Step Career Guide

Discover the complete pathway to becoming a registered migration agent in Australia. This guide covers OMARA registration requirements, the Graduate Diploma, Capstone exam, and career prospects for 2026.

Australia’s migration advice profession continues to grow in demand, with the Department of Home Affairs processing over 7.6 million temporary and permanent visa applications in the 2024–25 program year. For those seeking a regulated, intellectually rewarding career, becoming a registered migration agent (RMA) offers a clear professional pathway. The Office of the Migration Agents Registration Authority (OMARA) reports there are approximately 5,200 registered agents currently practising across Australia, a figure that has remained relatively stable despite rising visa complexity. This guide sets out the 2026 registration requirements, qualification steps, and practical considerations for anyone serious about entering the profession.

Understanding the Role of a Registered Migration Agent

A registered migration agent is legally authorised to provide immigration assistance and advice to visa applicants under the Migration Act 1958. This includes preparing visa applications, lodging appeals with the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART), and representing clients in dealings with the Department of Home Affairs. Unlike education agents or paralegals, only RMAs can charge fees for immigration advice in Australia.

The role demands a combination of legislative knowledge, ethical judgment, and client management skills. Agents interpret complex migration regulations, assess eligibility against constantly updated legislative instruments, and manage client expectations through often lengthy processing times. Many RMAs specialise in specific visa subclasses—employer-sponsored visas, partner visas, or protection visas—while others maintain a general practice. The work can be conducted independently, within a law firm, or alongside other professional services such as accounting or recruitment.

OMARA Registration Requirements for 2026

The Office of the Migration Agents Registration Authority (OMARA) sets the regulatory framework for entry into the profession. As of 2026, the core requirements for initial registration include:

  • Australian citizenship, permanent residency, or an eligible New Zealand visa—applicants must hold one of these statuses to apply for registration
  • Completion of an approved qualification—the Graduate Diploma in Australian Migration Law and Practice, or an equivalent OMARA-approved course
  • Successful completion of the Capstone Examination—a high-stakes assessment administered by the College of Law on behalf of OMARA
  • English language proficiency—demonstrated through citizenship, tertiary study in English, or an IELTS Academic score of at least 7.0 in each band
  • Fit and proper person assessment—including a National Police Certificate and professional conduct history review
  • Professional indemnity insurance—mandatory coverage before registration can be granted

Registration must be renewed annually, with agents required to complete 10 Continuing Professional Development (CPD) points each year, including at least one point in ethics.

The Graduate Diploma in Australian Migration Law and Practice

The Graduate Diploma in Australian Migration Law and Practice is the principal academic pathway to registration. Several Australian universities and registered training organisations offer this qualification, typically over a 12-month full-time or 18-month part-time structure. Providers approved by OMARA for 2026 include Griffith University, Victoria University, Murdoch University, and the Australian National University, among others.

The curriculum covers foundational units such as Australia’s visa system, migration legislation and policy, professional ethics, and client representation skills. Students learn to navigate the Migration Act 1958, Migration Regulations 1994, and the Department’s Procedural Advice Manual (PAM3). Assessment is typically competency-based, with written submissions, case studies, and simulated client interviews forming the bulk of evaluation tasks.

Tuition fees for the Graduate Diploma range from AUD $12,000 to $18,000 for domestic students in 2026, depending on the provider. Commonwealth Supported Places (CSP) are available at some universities, reducing the student contribution significantly. Prospective students should verify OMARA’s current list of approved courses before enrolling, as provider status can change.

The Capstone Examination: Structure and Preparation

The Capstone Examination remains the single most challenging hurdle for aspiring migration agents. Administered three times per year by the College of Law, it tests applied knowledge across the entire migration advice framework. The exam consists of multiple-choice questions, short-answer scenarios, and a substantial case-study component requiring candidates to prepare a complete visa application strategy.

Key areas assessed include:

  • Visa application procedures and decision-making
  • Code of Conduct obligations under the Migration Agents Regulations
  • Client file management and record-keeping
  • Refusal, cancellation, and merits review processes
  • Ethical dilemmas and professional boundaries

The pass rate for the Capstone has historically ranged between 55% and 70%, reflecting its difficulty. Candidates are permitted three attempts before being required to re-enrol in the Graduate Diploma. Preparation typically involves dedicated study over 8 to 12 weeks, often using commercial exam-preparation resources or study groups. The College of Law provides a detailed curriculum outline and sample questions, which should form the backbone of any study plan.

Registration Application and Initial Practice Steps

Once the qualification and Capstone are complete, the formal registration application is lodged through the OMARA online portal. The application fee for first-time registration in 2026 is approximately AUD $1,970, with a reduced fee for subsequent renewals. Processing times vary but generally take four to six weeks if all documentation is in order.

Newly registered agents must consider their practice structure. Options include:

  • Sole practice—offering the greatest autonomy but requiring business management skills and marketing investment
  • Employment with an established migration firm—providing mentorship and a steady client flow during the early years
  • In-house roles within corporations, universities, or government agencies—often focused on a narrower range of visa types

The Migration Institute of Australia (MIA) and the Migration Alliance both offer professional support, networking opportunities, and CPD events tailored to new practitioners. Membership is not mandatory but widely regarded as valuable for professional development and credibility.

Career Outlook and Earning Potential

Demand for migration services correlates strongly with Australia’s net overseas migration levels, which Treasury forecasts project at approximately 260,000 per annum through 2026–27. Employer-sponsored visa pathways, particularly in healthcare, technology, and construction, continue to generate steady advisory work. The expansion of the Pacific Australia Labour Mobility (PALM) scheme and ongoing reforms to the points-tested skilled migration system suggest sustained demand for qualified agents.

Income varies significantly by practice model and specialisation. A salaried migration agent in their first three years can expect earnings between AUD $70,000 and $95,000, while experienced agents in private practice frequently earn AUD $120,000 to $180,000. Sole practitioners with established client bases and niche expertise—such as complex employer sponsorship or Administrative Review Tribunal appeals—may exceed these ranges substantially.

It is worth noting that migration advice is a counter-cyclical profession. During periods of policy tightening or economic uncertainty, demand for professional advice often increases as visa pathways become more complex and refusal rates rise. This dynamic provides a degree of resilience not found in all professional services.

Maintaining Registration and Professional Growth

Annual renewal requires agents to demonstrate compliance with CPD obligations, maintain professional indemnity insurance, and confirm their continued fitness to practise. OMARA conducts periodic audits of agent files and practices, particularly where complaints have been lodged or where agents have been registered for less than three years.

Many RMAs pursue additional specialisations over time. The Accredited Specialist in Immigration Law credential, offered by the Law Society in several states, is available to agents who also hold a practising certificate as a legal practitioner. Others develop expertise in emerging areas such as the Global Talent Visa program, employer compliance audits, or the intersection of migration and family law.

For those who entered the profession through the Graduate Diploma pathway, there is also the option to articulate into a Master of Migration Law or a Juris Doctor at a later stage, broadening career options into full legal practice. Several universities offer advanced standing for completed migration law subjects, reducing the total study duration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I become a migration agent if I am not an Australian citizen?

You must hold Australian citizenship, permanent residency, or an eligible New Zealand visa to apply for OMARA registration. If you do not hold one of these statuses, you will need to obtain it before lodging your registration application, regardless of your qualifications.

How long does the entire process take from start to registration?

For someone starting from scratch, the typical timeline is 18 to 24 months. This includes 12 months for the Graduate Diploma, 2 to 3 months of Capstone preparation, and 4 to 6 weeks for OMARA processing. Part-time study extends this accordingly.

Is the Graduate Diploma available online?

Yes, several OMARA-approved providers offer the Graduate Diploma entirely online. This includes Victoria University and Griffith University, both of which have well-established distance-learning platforms. Online study is particularly common among students balancing work and family commitments.

What is the difference between a migration agent and an immigration lawyer?

A migration agent is registered with OMARA and can provide immigration advice and representation. An immigration lawyer holds a current practising certificate as a legal practitioner and is also entitled to provide immigration assistance under separate legislative provisions. Lawyers are not required to be OMARA-registered, though many choose to hold both credentials. The practical scope of work overlaps significantly, though lawyers may also handle judicial review applications in the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia.

Do I need to complete the Capstone if I am already a lawyer?

Lawyers who hold an unrestricted practising certificate are exempt from the Graduate Diploma and Capstone requirements for OMARA registration. However, they must still meet all other registration criteria, including the fit and proper person assessment and professional indemnity insurance requirements.
